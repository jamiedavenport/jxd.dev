"Weeks, not quarters" invites a fair question: what gets cut? The honest answer is nothing that matters. The speed comes from removing waste, not from removing rigour.

## Where the time actually goes

Most software projects do not spend their months writing code. They spend them on setup, coordination, rework, and waiting. Environments take weeks to stand up. Decisions queue behind meetings. Requirements drift because nobody has seen working software yet.

We attack each of those directly. Client work starts from our full-stack template, so auth, billing, deploys, and monitoring are solved before the first commit. There are no layers between you and the engineer, so decisions take a conversation, not a steering committee. And because working software ships in the first week, requirements get corrected by reality early, when the fix is cheap.

## What AI-native means here

We use AI tooling the way a good workshop uses power tools: everywhere it is faster, never where it is sloppier. Models draft, engineers decide. Every line still passes review, tests, and CI before it reaches production.

The result is that senior judgement gets amplified rather than replaced. One experienced engineer with modern tooling covers ground that used to take a small team, without the coordination overhead that made the team slow in the first place.

Rigour is not the thing you trade away for speed. Done properly, it is where the speed comes from.
