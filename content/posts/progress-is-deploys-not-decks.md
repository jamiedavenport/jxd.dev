If a project needs a weekly slide deck to prove it is moving, it is usually not moving. Decks exist to represent progress. Deploys are progress.

This is why every JXD engagement ships to production in the first week and keeps shipping every week after. Not because small releases are fashionable, but because they change what a status update is. The update becomes a link: here is what went live, click it.

## What replaces the deck

A short written note, once a week. What shipped, what went wrong, what is next, and what it cost. The problems section is not optional. Real projects hit problems weekly, and a note that never mentions any is describing a fictional project.

Written notes beat meetings for the same reason deploys beat decks: they are artefacts. They can be re-read, forwarded to a board, and checked against what actually happened. A meeting evaporates. A note accumulates into a history of the engagement.

None of this is complicated. It just requires being comfortable with the client seeing everything, which is the point.
