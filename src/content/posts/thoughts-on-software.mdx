I've been writing software for a long time and built a lot of products. During that time a lot has changed but not always for the better.

This is a writeup of my thoughts on what great software looks like. I use this document as my own guide to building software.

# Specific

Good software should do one thing and do it well. Having a narrow focus is something we teach to startup founders during their early stages.

Over time products grow, adding more and more features. This doesn't mean that the core functionality gets worse, but it can result in the experience feeling bloated and at worst you end up paying for features you don't need.

I hope the future has less products with taglines like "Changing the way you work" and more products with taglines like "A really good todo list".

# Accessible

I strongly believe that if you have something that solves a problem then you should make it accessible to the people that need it.

Over time I've found myself priced out of tools that I need more often. It makes sense because companies want to provide good returns for their investors and pay their employees well.

As technology, especially AI, gets better we should be able to do more with a lot less and therefore provide customers with cost savings. We are coming to the era of the one person $1B valuation so it only makes sense that software can be provided at a lower cost.

## Pricing Parity

We live in a globally connected world where people come from all walks of life. To make software truly accessible we need to provide it at a price that is fair for everyone regardless of their purchasing power.

Tools such as [ParityDeals](https://www.paritydeals.com/) can be used to localise pricing based on the user's location and integrates nicely with billing providers like Stripe and Polar.

## One-Time Purchases

Subscriptions have become the default pricing model for all SaaS companies. In the past we'd pay for software once and own it. Now we get billed regularly for software we forgot to cancel.

Going forward I want to see more one-time purchases for software or at least software cheap enough that paying yearly is a no-brainer.
